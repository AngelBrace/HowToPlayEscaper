Overview

The Escaper is a 3D first-person adventure. Explore the world in your cage, collect gear, fight enemies, and play optional arcade mini-games scattered around the map. You have 3 lives; landing in Ocean-tagged water cancels fall impact.

Game modes

Pick a mode from Game Selection → Game Mode, or in F2 Escaper Settings (Game mode dropdown). The HUD shows the active mode; Photon lobbies sync it when the host starts.

Live multiplayer session: after a Play / Explore run is already going, the host can pick another Play venue or Explore from Game Mode. Everyone stays in the same Photon room. The old mini-game ends — the Ray Racer bike is removed, and hockey bots are destroyed — then everyone is moved to the new game. That game is reset to a lobby. A match that was already going does not continue, and the new one does not start by itself. The host’s OPS for that game opens so the host can press Start. Chess resets the board at the stele. Explore only moves everyone. Combat modes (Modern / Quest / Classic / Infinite) stay a full New Game. New Game on your own machine does the same cleanup before the next mode starts, and it does not restart the other rider. Guests cannot switch. Overlays such as Game Selection close so everyone returns to the 3DGameHUD; leftover Game Info balloon text from the previous venue is cleared. The other rider’s bike follows a short moment behind, so the motion stays smooth.

The Escaper (Modern)

Default full adventure: enemy waves, random lost Cage Bowl and Propeller spawns, SoundSight treasure chimes, then find the Duck Raft and escape Arcade Island.

Escaper (Quest)

LavaFlow crossing: At the base of the southeast mountains, LavaFlow is a field of random floating lava rocks. Each rock is stable for only a few seconds (about 3) before it bubbles and sinks into the flow. A bloop warning sounds around 2 seconds — listen for it and leap to the next rock before yours drops. Sunk rocks rise back up so you can retry the crossing.

Same combat and escape goal as Modern, but quest items sit at fixed landmarks. The lost Propeller is on SnowyLavaRock — the odd snow-covered rock in the middle of LavaFlow that does not sink (unlike the floating lava rocks around it). A Snowman stands on the rock; behind the Snowman is the beige Large Ammo Crate (the green Large Ammo Crate with the DetPack is in CrossFields). Stand on SnowyLavaRock for +1 health and +1 fuel every second (Sleigh Ride MIDI loops while you are there). The beige crate stockpiles yellow ammo while you stay on the rock — one ball on first contact, then another every 15 seconds (the timer pauses if you leave); walk to the crate to collect. After the Propeller, the Cage Bowl is fixed high on a skyscraper — you need flight to reach it.

Infinite Enemies

Like Modern, but enemy amount sliders are hidden and waves keep coming until you destroy 8 island sentry buoys (then spawning stops). Destroy 3 sentries to disable JellyRover boss spawn, same as other modes.

Escaper Classic (The Prisoner)

No grunt waves — only the JellyRover boss, which hunts you starting 30 seconds after the run begins (and keeps respawning). Still find bowl, propeller, and duck raft to escape.

Explore Arcade Island

No enemies; Cage Bowl, Propeller, and Duck Raft are already equipped. Roam the island and play any mini-game without the main quest pressure.

Play Checkers / Chess / BaffleShots / Bowling / Pool / Teeny Tiny Tetris / Connect 4 / Ray Racer / Hockey Arena

No enemies; quest gear, full ammo, and full fuel from the start. Each mode warps you to its venue (North Parthenon checkers, South Parthenon chess, Nav OPS East/West, bowling lane, billiard table, Tetris South/North OPS, Connect 4 south of the Cooling Towers, the Ray Racer deck off the island, or the Hockey Arena rink) so you can practice or host multiplayer there. In a live Photon session the host can hop between these venues (and Explore) without ending the room — see Live multiplayer session above.

Enemies

Ground enemies roll toward you. Flying ones stay in the air and shoot. With Sky Eye (F11) open: Shift+1 JellyRover, Shift+2 Zombie, Shift+3 Normal, Shift+4 Purple. Orange has no Sky Eye key.

FlipChip (coin toss)

Two half-checkers (red + black) with their own rigidbody and bounce. Melee object pickup / toss is a core Melee function and stays available on the CheckerBoard, cue stand, and other no-weapon / mini-game zones.

  • Equip 1 Melee. Aim at a FlipChip and press RMB (no Shift) to pick it up. RMB again drops it.
  • Mouse wheel flips the held chip. Shift+wheel still sets Punch for the toss.
  • LMB while holding tosses it. Punch scales throw power, spin, and hit damage.
  • A thrown chip that hits an enemy deals melee-style damage and knockback. After it settles, the HUD shows Red Side Up or Black Side Up.
  • Console: /flipchip drops a new chip 10 ahead and 50 up.

Venue FlipChip (seats): Each terminal has its own button — checkersFlipChipButton, chessFlipChipButton, bowlingFlipChipButton, poolFlipChipButton, connect4FlipChipButton. One toss before Start. No toss: the host is Player 1 and moves first. Black side up: the host stays Player 1. Red side up: the guest takes the Player 1 seat and moves first. A solo toss is a free chip and does not move anyone. Reset lets you toss again (Checkers keeps its color until the next toss). Tetris and BaffleShots have no seat toss. Checkers also uses the coin for color: black side up → host plays Black; red side up → host plays Red (default Black). Chip lost in the ocean → press that venue's FlipChip button again. While the coin is in the world, the FlipChip icon under the compass points toward it — see Direction indicators (hidden while held in melee). The same icon tracks forRealFlipChip, the LED coin.

LED coin in hand: one RGB LED inside the chip. Green face up → whole chip glows green. Red face up → whole chip glows red. On its edge → blue. Use the mouse wheel to pick the face. RMB drop keeps that glow. LMB toss flashes green/red at random until it stops — you will not know the call until it lands. Green is Player 1; red is Player 2; blue on edge plays the Game Selection theme.

Blue Angel (North Aerie): Stand in the angel zone for one continuous minute (leaving resets the timer; the visit count does not). A flashing green/red coin appears above the angel and the compass icon follows it. Take it with Melee nearby (LMB, or RMB). If a coin is already hovering, a later minute does not pay ammo until you take it. After it is taken, a completed minute grants 3–5 yellow ammo. Console: /irlFlipChip.

C4 Timer Bomb

Collect the DetPack from the light-green Large Ammo Crate in CrossFields (LargeAmmoCrate on the map). Until then, yellow GameBalls bounce off you. After DetPack, each yellow collect is +1 C4 charge. A fourth inventory icon turns green when the DetPack is found — there is no compass direction arrow for the crate or DetPack (only the four indicators listed under Direction indicators).

  • 9 toggles Blaster Grenade and C4TimerBomb (after DetPack). Quick Menu C4TimerBomb also works. Needs at least one C4 charge. Slot shows DetPack icon plus charges · fuse (e.g. 3x · 10s).
  • Throw power uses Melee Punch — set on weapon 1 (wheel / Shift+wheel). C4 does not change Punch; it reuses whatever you last set on the fist.
  • Mouse wheel in C4 mode sets fuse length (3s min, 30s max; default 10s). Fuse meter flashes under the sight; bomb LCD shows the preset before stick.
  • LMB quick throw — spawns and throws at your aim (no RMB hold required).
  • RMB hold — arm in hand (−1 charge). LMB while holding = throw. Release in open air drops it so it falls and sticks. Release while it is already against a surface plants it there. The held bomb does not pass through solids. Fuse starts when it sticks.
  • Blast radius is 2× Blaster Grenade; chain-detonates other armed C4 in range.
  • First solid hit sticks. The bomb LCD counts 10:00 down to 00:00 in 60ths of a second. The 3D DetonatorLight mesh flashes GreenEmit / RedEmit (faster in the last 2 seconds). An electronic beep plays at 10:00 and every second until detonation. Stay too close and you take blast damage. Blast uses the BaffleShots ship-hit VFX (CFXR2 WW Explosion).
  • Blast radius is 2x the grenade blast. Damages enemies and the player, knocks rigidbodies, and destroys SentryBuoys (they sink). 3 sentries still disable JellyRover boss spawn; 8 still stop Infinite waves.
  • Console: /c4 spawns one in front of you (debug). /giveall grants DetPack.

Throw weapons — Cannon Ball & BallChain

Both share the movable throw sight loft (mouse wheel / gamepad LT–RT). Each weapon remembers its own loft angle when you switch away and back. Cannon uses the green sight + degree text; BallChain uses the purple sight + degree text.

WeaponKeySightPlay
Cannon Ball 4 Green throw sight Higher-speed toss; becomes a persistent ammo ball
BallChain 0 Purple throw sight Low-speed cannon-style toss that stamps a climbable ball ladder as it flies
  • BallChain steps are solid as soon as each ball is laid — climb while the lead is still in the air.
  • Keep shooting to extend or rebuild if an unfinished trail times out and clears.
  • Only a few chain lines stay in the world at once (oldest are removed).
  • Melee a chain segment to destroy that line.
  • Each BallChain toss costs several ammo.

Equipment

  • F — Propeller (flight). While the engine is on, sliders under the circle map show THRUST, RPM, HOVER, HEIGHT (full = 1.5 km), and VERTICAL, each with its number. The green altitude at the top of the screen stays. The panel hides when the engine is off.
  • B — Duck Raft / Bowl
  • E — Interact / collect. Off the island, E mounts or dismounts a Ray Racer bike you have already found. It does nothing if you have not found one. On the Ray Racer deck, E opens or closes the RayRacerOPS lobby. You stay on the bike and on the deck.

Menus

  • ESC — Pause / open Game Selection (3D HUD off). On the Ray Racer deck, if RayRacerOPS is open, the first ESC only closes that panel and leaves you on the deck. ESC again opens Game Selection. During death or victory, toggles the menu; ESC again returns to the camera pan
  • P — Game pause (blocked during death / victory)
  • Tab — Quick Menu (weapons / items). On the checkers or chess board: 2D board overlay instead
  • ` or F9 — Console
  • Enter / Space — Confirm in menus (Game Over uses the on-screen Continue / Restart buttons)

Quick Menu navigation

  • W / A / S / D — Direction categories. After DetPack, C4TimerBomb sits next to BallChain on the left/horizontal row; selecting it shows the yellow-ball charge count.
  • E / Q — Cycle items in the selected direction
  • LMB — Select (single-fire for auto weapons)
  • RMB — Select (auto-fire mode)
  • S+A held — Exit to Game Selection

Time control

Pause / Break toggles slow motion while you play. Page Up / Page Down raise or lower time scale. This is separate from P (normal game pause).

Lives, death, and water

You start with 3 lives. A lethal hit (enemies, fall onto land or buildings, void) costs one life. The world snapshot-pauses: you stay frozen while the camera orbits and the Game Over panel is up.

SituationPanelButton
Lives left (e.g. 2/3) You were Killed! plus the reason and lives remaining Continue — respawn and keep the run
Last life GAME OVER! plus the reason and final score Restart — full new game
Escaped the island Victory! Restart — new game

GAME OVER appears only when all lives are gone. Mid-run deaths do not stamp GAME OVER on the HUD toast; the panel already has the reason and lives.

ESC during the end sequence

  1. First ESC — Game Selection (3D HUD off), same as a normal pause.
  2. Second ESC — back to the orbit and Game Over panel, still paused, with Continue or Restart restored.
  3. P does nothing here. Click the button to leave the sequence.

Water landing (Ocean)

Landing in Ocean-tagged water (rivers and sea at the water line) cancels fall impact, like Minecraft water. You do not need the propeller. Solid ground, buildings, and the void still hurt. Outer ocean on the Duck Raft is still the escape / victory path.

Tip Fall into a river or the sea without the propeller and you survive the landing. Hitting dirt or a rooftop from the same height still costs health (and a life if it kills you).

Direction indicators (under the compass)

Below the compass, up to four sliding icons show where something important is on the map. Quest-item icons appear clockwise in the order you collect them (bowl, then propeller, then duck raft). FlipChip uses the same band but is not a quest item — it points at the Checkers coin while it is in the world. Each icon moves left or right on the band to match compass bearing — center means straight ahead. Icons grow larger when you are close and shrink as you move away (about full size near 1 m, smallest around 1 km). If the target is far behind you, its icon hides until you turn back toward it.

IconPoints towardWhen it shows

Each icon sits in a matching bracket ring (two half-circles). The ring stays the same size; the icon grows as you get closer and shrinks as you move away.

The three quest icons can also pulse when SoundSight hears their treasure chime. The DetPack and ammo crates use the top inventory row only — there is no compass direction icon for them.

Mini-games — shared lobby & turns

Yellow GameOPS zone

Every GameOPS terminal sits on a yellow grid. Step onto it and that game’s UI turns on. Weapons do not fire — mouse or gamepad clicks the terminal instead. Step off and combat works again.

Same idea everywhere: Nav OPS, Pool, Bowling, Tetris OPS, and Checkers OPS. Chess-Stele has two yellow pads — front and back. Ray Racer and Hockey Arena do not use a yellow grid. Ray Racer’s lobby is a HUD panel on the deck, and the whole deck is weapons-free. Hockey’s lobby is Hockey Arena OPS, opened with E while you are inside any collider on the arena.

Two extras (not the GameOPS menu): Tetris Play Spot also turns weapons off, but that pad is for moving pieces, not the OPS menu. On the Checkers board, standing on a piece or looking down also turns weapons off so hops can use RMB.

Multiplayer lobby

Host is the player who started the room. Only the host opens the lobby, changes mode, resets, and starts.

Remotes can only Join and open Settings for their own music volume — they cannot open the lobby, change mode, reset, or start. The host runs the table / lane.

Whose turn / who won? Everyone sees "{PlayerName}'s Turn" on the large top HUD for about 7 seconds, then it fades. Pool also shows "{PlayerName} Wins!" on that header when a match ends. Press Caps Lock anytime to show the same info in the game info balloon. Live standings are on F8 scores pages. Space cycles Escaper, Bowling, Billiards, BaffleShots, Chess, Tetris, Checkers, Connect 4, Ray Racer, and Hockey.

Tetris exception: Tetris is live / simultaneous — it does not use turn banners. 2P flashes Players Ready when Playing starts (host South Play Spot 5s after Engage).

Who rides: a room can be larger than the match. The lobby size goes up to 16. Ray Racer plays the host and the first three others. Hockey plays the host and the first seven. Anyone past that is not spawned and is not on the rink or the deck. A bar at the bottom of the screen has Previous, Escaper, and Next. Previous and Next follow people who are actually playing. Escaper looks over the island. When a seat opens, the camera goes back to their cage and they are sent to that game’s start. The other mini-games still let the whole room play.

Game Roster sits on the HUD. Ray Racer fills the bars it has riders for. Solo, with the cursor free, right-click a Ray Racer bar to open that bike’s color list. Hockey uses all eight bars: Team A on the first four, Team B on the last four. Empty hockey bars say Open. Until the host rearranges them, the host is the Team A captain on the top bar and the first other rider is the Team B captain on the first bottom bar. The host can drag a name from one side to the other while the cursor is free. Drop it on a name to swap. Drop it on Open to move. Each header shows that side’s captain nickname in the captain’s ray color. An empty side stays Team A or Team B.

Score history: F8 is the pause scores panel. The 3D GameScoreBoard at the south of the island is a separate history of solo and multiplayer scores, including Ray Racer and Hockey. It does not open the F8 panel. A score stays on the computer that recorded it until those players share a room; after that, both machines keep the merged list, so a visitor can look up scores from the other computer.

Room chat

In a Photon room, G opens a blue panel at the bottom left. Enter sends. Esc closes. The cursor is free and the bike does not steer while the panel is open. A plain line goes to everyone in the room, including spectators. You see it as YourNick: hello.

@Nick hello is a private whisper. Only you and that rider see it. On Twitch, @Nick is a public mention; here it is not. During a hockey match, @ATeam, @BTeam, and @team go only to that side. @team is your own side. Outside a hockey match those words are ordinary room text.

/me waves shows as an action. /who lists the room. :puck:, :wave:, and :star: are the emotes. Pasted emoji do not draw.

The host can /ban @Nick and /unban @Nick. A ban kicks that rider and keeps them out of this room until they are unbanned. You cannot ban yourself, and a guest who types /ban gets Host only. /ignore @Nick hides that person’s chat on your computer only. It does not remove their bike. /unignore @Nick brings it back. /bans and /ignores list those. The same commands work in the F9 console. When a line arrives, that rider’s Game Roster bar flashes.

Modes

  • Free — sink any ball in any order; keep the turn after a legal sink (no foul). Miss when balls stop → next player (No balls pocketed — not a foul).
  • 8-Ball Last — sink others first; early 8 in the bay scores no points / can lose.
  • Solids & Stripes — first group ball sunk locks groups per player (board shows Name (Solids) / Name (Stripes)). Wrong group / early 8 = no points.
  • 9-Ball Arcade — diamond rack (1 at apex, 9 in center); 9 must be last except a golden break (9 on a legal opening break = win). Early 9 otherwise = loss.
  • 9-Ball Classic — same diamond; always hit the lowest ball first; pocket the 9 on any legal shot to win. Illegal 9 → foul + 9 spotted.

Cue-stand shooting

  1. Walk onto the cue ball to select it (purple sight + cue-stick power slider).
  2. Aim with look (banks / open aim OK — no object ball required under the ray).
  3. Mouse wheel sets shot power; LMB shoots a flat impulse.
  4. Space / Jump (or step off the cue) cancels select. Weapons are halted while the cue is selected.
  5. The cue is not melee-punched — only cue-stand shots move it. Object balls can still be nudged with weapons / melee when not on the cue.

Break / ball-in-hand

  • Opening break: cue starts in the kitchen (behind the head string).
  • After a cue scratch (or certain fouls): ball-in-hand — stand on the cue, hold Shift, aim a transparent ghost, LMB to place once. Release Shift to cancel (cue stays put).
  • Most modes: place in the kitchen. 9-Ball fouls: place anywhere on the felt.

Turns, scratch, scoring

After you hit, the table waits until every ball in motion fully stops. Legal sink + no foul → keep turn; miss or foul → next player.

Scratch / foul (important split):

  • Cue scratch (foul + re-spot + ball-in-hand): cue goes down a pocket into the return bay, or stays off the felt for about 3 seconds. Brief pocket airtime is fine. Cage bumps that shove the cue are not treated as pocket scratches. A ball you legally pocketed on that same shot still scores. The cue scratch does not turn it into a no-points sink.
  • Object ball off the table (~3s off both felt and bay, e.g. on the floor): scratched / no points (red X on the header). The cue is not re-spotted and may keep rolling until it stops — that alone is not ball-in-hand.
  • A ball confirmed in the return bay is a sink (green check on the header). Wrong group, an early 8, or a ball that already left the table is Sunk (illegal, no points) or Sunk (was scratched, no points) — that reason is the ball, not the cue.

HUD when the balls stop:

  • Cue down a pocket or off the table: everyone sees one line. Cue only: Cue scratched. Cue plus a ball that scored: Name sunk Solids #3 — cue scratched. Cue plus a ball that already scored nothing: Sunk (illegal, no points) Solids #3 — cue scratched.
  • The player who places the cue gets that same line plus stand on cue + SHIFT to move once (9-ball: anywhere). The other player does not get the place instruction.
  • A 9-ball win names the player (Name pocketed the 9, Name — golden break!). The header is Name Wins!. An early 8 in solo is Name lost; in multiplayer the opponent gets Name Wins!.
  • Solo miss: Foul or Legal break (the turn does not say it passed). Two players: Foul — turn passed / Legal break — turn passed.
  • Shooting before Start: host Start the match first, guest Waiting for host to start. During a match, the other player sees Not your turn (Pool).
  • Cue still rolling: Wait for the cue to stop.

Header icons: the table header shows all 15 ball icons. Green check = sunk for credit; red X = scratched / no-points.

Big Dumb Bowling

Stand on the yellow grid at the lane.

Play Bowling (lobby / venue start) seats host/solo at BowlingOPS1 and Player 2 at BowlingOPS2 (facing BowlingOPS). When the match starts, both cages warp onto BowlingPlatform (BowlingPlayer1 / BowlingPlayer2; solo uses Player 1).

During a match, only the current thrower can fire weapons while inside bowlingPlayZone on BowlingPlatform (the raised pad with steps; the volume is tall enough to jump for trick shots). The other player is blocked until their turn.

InputWhoAction
Start + LMBHost / soloOffline: 10-frame game. In a room: rack pins and wait
Join + LMBRemoteJoin — the match starts
Reset + LMBHostLobby reset

Player 2 aims Join on the OPS panel (no Shift, no RMB).

Each player bowls a full frame (up to two balls, or three in the 10th; a strike ends early), then the turn passes. Scoreboard and F8 bowling page show roster and turn.

One shot per ball, exactly like a real roll. Every time the pins reset and it is your turn you get one shot — it counts whatever it does, and you cannot shoot again until the pins reset, no matter how many are left standing. That gives you 2 balls per frame and 3 in the 10th. A shot that topples nothing (including a graze that only rattles the rack) is a gutterball: it scores 0, is marked - on the card, and the lane calls Gutterball on screen for 7 seconds before fading.

A gutterball on ball 1 does not end the frame — the pins cycle and you still get ball 2 at the remaining rack, so spares are still available. Two gutterballs close the frame as an open frame. While a shot is live or the pins are cycling the trigger is blocked (Wait for your ball / Wait for the pins to reset). Jump trick shots remain legal; there is no foul line.

Tetris

Stand on the yellow grid at Tetris OPS for the menu (board/rules, Settings, Start, Reset). Host uses the South pad; guest uses North. The Play Spot is a separate no-weapon pad for moving pieces, not the OPS menu. Live standings also on F8 → Tetris.

Tetris is live / simultaneous — it does not use "{Name}'s Turn" (solo never flashes a turn banner). 2P flashes Players Ready when the match goes live. Ends use Wins! / You Lose! / Draw! / Co-op Clear! / Sprint Clear! / Time's Up!.

Clamping to a Tetris PlayZone block

After Start, clamp to one of the five PlayZone blocks in front of the Tetris OPS panel. South / Player 1 uses the blue blocks; North / Player 2 uses the red blocks. That is where piece control lives — not the yellow OPS grid.

You have 5 seconds to pick a block and settle. Clamping starts 3 seconds after you stop moving (if you were already standing there when the match went live, you clamp right away).

Once clamped, the cage cannot strafe or walk. All WASD (and the stick) moves the Tetris piece instead. Jump off a PlayZone block to break the clamp any time. You can jump up and land on a different block. Each time you leave a block during a match, stay still 3 seconds again before WASD returns to Tetris.

Match flow

  • Engage → South pad 5s → Playing (host). Already on your pad → clamp immediately; else 3s settle. Then 0.5s ready wait → first piece (A/D ready).
  • Marathon, Sprint, and Ultra are offline only. In a room the host can start only the four 2-player modes.
  • Marathon — solo well; sky score line. Survive as long as you can. Offline only.
  • Sprint — first to 40 lines; elapsed time; Sprint Clear!; top-out → Game Over.
  • Ultra — 2:00 clock; Time's Up! keeps score; top-out → Game Over.
  • 2P Co-op — shared 20×20 (no mid wall), two pieces. Team 40 lines → Co-op Clear!; either top-out → shared Game Over. Bright = yours, dim = other.
  • 2P Vs Race — dual 10×20 + mid wall. South = right well, North = left. First to 40 lines wins; top-out loses.
  • 2P Vs Attack — same dual layout. Garbage: Double 1, Triple 2, Tetris 4 (Single 0). Clears cancel your pending garbage first; leftover rows rise on the opponent’s next lock.
  • 2P Ultra Duel — same dual layout. Shared 2:00; highest score wins; tie → Draw!; top-out loses.
  • After Game Over / Vs end, blocks and TetrisBorder stay until host Start or Reset.

Playing on the 3D board

You must be inside the board play zone. Weapons and RMB zoom stay off whenever you touch a piece or square, or stand in the zone looking down. Play stays on the 3D board — stand, aim, RMB, LMB. Tab opens Quick2DCheckersBoard, a live 2D display of the same squares, pieces, Crown on kings, and markers (blue = cage square, green = legal / aimed, orange = pending only). Tab again closes it. It does not select or move pieces.

  1. Stand on one of your pieces to select it — a blue circle marks that square.
  2. Markers appear: green sphere (GreenEmit) = a square you can land on, green circle = a piece you can capture. The end of a multi-jump gets the raised far sphere.
  3. Aim a marker to preview that line; it brightens (still green). Aiming a middle hop takes only that much of the chain, aiming the far sphere takes the whole chain.
  4. RMB locks the preview as pending — orange sphere and circle, status reading Pending A3 -> C5 x2 (LMB commit).
  5. LMB commits and the piece hops square by square. Backspace cancels the pending line; RMB again clears the pending line and the selection.
Chains are binding. Mid-chain you are locked to the jumping piece — the status reads "Black must continue jumping", and neither Backspace nor leaving the zone releases it until the chain finishes. Otherwise, stepping out of the play zone clears your selection.

Ray Racer

Play Ray Racer is an off-island light-cycle match. Choosing it puts you on your section of the deck and on the bike. Up to four riders, one quadrant each: the host and the first three others. Anyone past that spectates (see Who rides above). The deck sits in a star box, away from Arcade Island. You stay on the bike until the match ends or you leave for another game. The whole deck is weapons-free: guns and melee do not fire there.

The match does not start by itself. A green READY button appears. Click it, or press Enter or gamepad A. In a room the button switches to WAITING until every rider has pressed Ready. Solo starts the countdown on that one press. E while you are waiting hides Ready and opens the lobby. Closing the lobby brings Ready back.

Then the bikes stay parked through GRID, a red 3, 2, 1, and green GO. Hold the gas from 2 through GO for a ROCKET START (a short boost). Gas on the red 3 is a BURNOUT (a short crawl). The engine hum follows your speed and goes quiet while the game is paused.

The match opens in third person. V (gamepad Back) cycles Normal, Full Screen, and Third Person. On the deck the number keys do not change weapons. 1 is the chase camera, 2 left, 3 right, 4 front, 5 top down. Opening the lobby does not snap the camera back.

E on the deck opens RayRacerOPS on the same camera. The rider stops so you can click, and the cursor unlocks. E again closes the panel and gives you the bike back. You do not leave the arena. ESC while the panel is open closes it and stays on the deck. ESC when the panel is already closed opens Game Selection, and that is the step that leaves.

Settings

Settings swaps the rules page and the settings page. The rules-page header shows the ride style.

  • Aim the mode button and roll the mouse wheel for Free Ray or Classic Tron.
  • Theme is host-only. Click it, or aim it and roll the wheel, to cycle Amber Circuit, Marble, Classic Tron, Violet Night, Emerald, and Chrome. That recolors the floor, the grid lines, the border, and the pillars.
  • The sky slider is host-only and picks one of six star boxes: Shine Blue, Shine Black, Nebula Blue, Nebula Black, Common Blue, Common Black.
  • The grid-line slider and the floor-fade slider are host-only and stay independent of the theme.
  • The music slider and the RayRacerBikeColor gallery are yours. The computer bike is always grey, and its ray is white.
  • Start and Reset are host-only. Both begin a fresh round from the section seats, show Ready again, and close the lobby.

Riding

  • W forward, S slow reverse, A / D steer.
  • Free Ray — mouse look also turns the bike.
  • Classic Tron — travel stays on the compass. A left or right press is saved, and the bike turns 90 degrees only when it reaches the next even grid line (every 2 units). Odd positions do not turn. The view eases into the new heading.
  • Hold Shift while going forward to boost. The engine overheats if you hold it too long, then cools before boost works again.
  • A wall, a pillar, or a light-cycle beam stops you. The other rider’s beam stops you too, including the piece still being drawn. Your own ribbon does not, until it is a finished wall. The camera orbits that rider while everyone else keeps moving, and the beam sinks.
  • Continue puts you back in the race where you stopped when a life remains, with no new countdown.
  • Restart on that panel starts another Ray Racer round and returns to Ready. It does not dump you to Game Selection.

The default match is one life, so a crash usually starts a new round. Console /tron sets one life for the next match. /raylives sets three lives. Off the deck, in a normal Escaper game, E mounts or dismounts a Ray Racer bike you have found. E does nothing if you have not found one. A bike parked on the GamePlane is scale 1. Scale 10 is only the socket on the Ray Racer deck.

On the bike

Weapons and zoom stay off during a live match on the rink.

  • Drive into the puck and it leaves along the nose. That is a straight nudge or slap. LMB kicks left. RMB kicks right.
  • Cruise speed is a nudge. Full speed is a slap, capped by the arena’s max puck speed. Between those, the kick grows with the bike.
  • You can ride up the curved sides of the rink. The other rider follows that path.
  • Bots do the same, and they ride the curve too. Lined up on the enemy net, the kick is straight. Off that line, they pick a side.
  • You and any online rider can leave or enter through the nets. Bots cannot drop below the ice.
  • A puck that leaves the play zone respawns at center. A shot that beats the goal mouth, or the stand just behind that mouth, scores for the other team.

Roster

Once E has freed the cursor, the host can drag a name between Team A and Team B. Drop it on a name to swap. Drop it on Open to move. Right-click a bar:

  • Open → Activate. That adds a bot on that team.
  • A bot → Color and Deactivate.
  • A real player has no menu.

Four riders a side, and the host counts as one. The even game is the host plus seven bots: three beside the host, four on the other side. In multiplayer the host can fill any seat that is not already a person, up to those same caps. A solo lineup (which side, which color) is remembered the next time you play. A multiplayer room can use that saved lineup. Changes you make in a room are not written back over it.

BaffleShots at Nav OPS

Step on the yellow grid at Nav OPS (weapons off). RMB zoom is blocked only during fleet setup / Nav Plan, not once battle starts. Your turn / Opponent's turn use the shared HUD banner and Caps Lock balloon. Status also appears on F8 → BaffleShots.

Planning

  • Nav Plan — random kick-start for your fleet groups (and Repulse Mines if enabled). Reposition with RMB drag · LMB rotate · release to place. Nav Plan hides once battle is underway; Reset returns to setup and shows it again.
  • Confirm / Start when the fleet is complete. Solo: Confirm starts vs AI. 2P: each seat Confirms. Host first → Waiting… until the guest confirms, then Start. Guest first → host button is already Start (one click locks the host fleet and begins battle). Only the host can Start; the guest stays on Waiting… / Ready.

Settings

  • Classic BattleShip — hardest. Hits stay secret as Group? and Ship? (you still see the picture) until you sink the whole group. Group Sink stays on.
  • BaffleShots — you see the ship name as soon as you hit it.
  • In both, a hit is an orange X and HIT!. When the group is gone, those marks turn red and it says SUNK!.
  • Group Sink — on: the whole group goes down together. Off: ships in the group can sink one at a time. (Classic keeps this on.)
  • Repulse Mines and Music are in the same panel (aim the music slider + wheel). In 2P, the host sets Classic, Group Sink, and mines. Music is just for you.

Repulse Mines

Experimental attack mines. When on, each side plants 2 on empty water on their fleet grid — the water the opponent is shooting at. Nav Plan places them randomly; move with RMB drag and LMB (or RMB-release) — setup only, not on ships or shot cells. You see the icon on your fleet grid; the target grid shows it only after a shot hits one (REPULSE, yellow X). The shot bounces back at the ship that fired it: that player takes a random remaining hull hit and must stop to repair for one turn (yellow X on that fleet cell + smoke; the opponent gets an extra shot). When their real turn returns, smoke and yellow X clear unless that ship was hit again during repairs.

Battle & game over

Aim the viewport crosshair at the target grid and LMB to fire on your turn. On game over, the target grid reveals any remaining enemy ships and unexploded Repulse Mines.

Solo: you vs AI when you are not in a multiplayer room. 2P: East is the host, West is the guest. Play BaffleShots (and a live venue switch) seats host East and guest West. Place and Confirm at your seat; host Start when both fleets are locked (see Confirm / Start above). Host-only Reset. Ocean fog hides the opponent’s ships until you hit them; hit and sink effects still play.
